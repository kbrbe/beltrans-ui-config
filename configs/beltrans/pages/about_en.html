Info about the BELTRANS database

Database

The BELTRANS database gathers information on book translations from Dutch to French or French to Dutch by Belgian authors, illustrators or editors, published anywhere in the world in the period 1970-2020. During the data curation process, we gave priority to four literary genres, namely poetry, novels, children’s literature and comics, as well as to history books.

The database contains not only bibliographic information on translations and their source titles but also information on actors (both persons and organisations) who contributed to book translations, in their roles as authors, illustrators, editors, translators or publishers.

A total of 15,087 translations are documented in the database, of which …% from French to Dutch and … % Dutch to French.

In addition to an overall search, the database can be accessed through three entry points:

  • Translations
  • Authors
  • Publishers

Information on the books in the database comprehends target and source titles, author, illustrator, editor, translator, publisher, year of publication, place and country of publication, the target and souce language(s), its genre or domain, …, ISBN, edition statement, in as far as it could be ascertained.

As to actors involved with book translations, this database contains information on:

  • authors, illustrators, editors and translators;
  • publishers and some other organisations.

Data sources

The bibliographic information in this database has been compiled from the following four sources:

  • Royal Library of Belgium (KBR): the General catalogue https://opac.kbr.be
  • National Library of the Netherlands (KB): the linked data publishing portal https://data.bibliotheken.nl
  • National Library of France (BnF): the Catalogue général https://catalogue.bnf.fr as well as datadumps (RDF/XML) of the linked data publishing portal https://data.bnf.fr
  • Unesco – Index Translationum: via the search interface (now offline and replaced by a downloadable dataset)

Other data sources were used indirectly in order to identify and enrich records from the KBR catalogue. Namely:

  • The Vertalingendatabase

In addition, we have also integrated data from the following databases to provide information on authors:

  • International Standard Name Identifier (ISNI): https://isni.org via SRU Search and Linked Data dump
  • Virtual Internet Authority File (VIAF): https://viaf.org via Linked Data dump
  • Wikidata: https://www.wikidata.org via the Wikidata Query Service

The detailed pages always indicate the provenance of the information and let you click on identifiers to view the online source records (except for UNESCO records).

Disclaimer

This database is the result of a four-year research project. While we aimed to make the information as complete and high-quality as possible, given the limited timeframe and resources available, we cannot guarantee that the database is exhaustive or free from error.

When curating the data, priority was given to genres that have been studied both quantitatively and qualitatively, namely poetry, novels, children’s literature, comics, and history books. Furthermore, our focus was primarily on information about the translations, with details about the source titles being supplemented subsequently, which meant that this information remained less complete.

In the interests of transparency and verifiability, we specify the source of the data in each instance so that you can click through to the catalogue description from which we obtained the information.

If you notice any errors, please report them via beltrans@kbr.be. We will not be able to correct the errors within this database immediately. After all, the BELTRANS database is a static academic dataset which was finalised in October 2025, although new versions may be published at a later date. If the error concerns KBR, ISNI or Wikidata, however, we can amend the relevant record immediately in the KBR catalogue, ISNI database or Wikidata. If the error concerns KB (the Netherlands) or BnF (France), we will inform our colleagues accordingly. Unfortunately, there is no reporting mechanism for errors in the Unesco - Index Translationum or VIAF.

Research project

The BELTRANS research project (2021-2025) studied the untold history of literary translation flows in Belgium between French and Dutch in the period 1970–2020. It resulted from the cooperation between the Royal Library of Belgium (KBR) and the universities KU Leuven and UCLouvain. More information can be found on the BELTRANS-webpage and the KBR GitHub.
